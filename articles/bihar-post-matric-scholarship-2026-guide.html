Bihar Post Matric Scholarship 2026-27: Complete Guide for Kosi Division Students

Updated 30 August 2026 · Scholarships

This guide explains the process and links to official sources as of 30 August 2026. Deadlines, income ceilings and scholarship amounts are revised by the state government from time to time, and outdated figures circulate widely online. Always confirm final details on the official government portal linked below before you act.

If you are a student from Saharsa, Madhepura or Supaul studying beyond class 10, the Bihar Post Matric Scholarship (PMS) is the largest source of education funding available to you — and the application window is open right now. It closes on 15 September 2026.

This guide covers who qualifies, which of the two portals you need, what documents to arrange before you sit down at the computer, how much money you can expect, and the specific mistakes that get Kosi division applications rejected every year.

First: which portal do you use?

This trips up more students than anything else, so settle it before you do anything else. Bihar runs the Post Matric Scholarship through two separate portals, split by category:

  • BC and EBC students — apply on pmsonline.bihar.gov.in
  • SC and ST students — apply on scstpmsonline.bihar.gov.in
Official portals: pmsonline.bihar.gov.in for BC/EBC, and scstpmsonline.bihar.gov.in for SC/ST. These are the only official application sites. Any site asking you to pay a registration fee to apply is not one of them.

Registering on the wrong portal is not a small error. The application does not transfer across, and by the time it is caught during verification the deadline may have passed. Check your caste certificate, confirm your category, then pick the portal.

Eligibility

  • Permanent resident of Bihar — this includes all three Kosi division districts: Saharsa, Madhepura and Supaul.
  • Belongs to the SC, ST, BC or EBC category, with a valid caste certificate.
  • Annual family income (parent or guardian, including the student's own income) within the prescribed ceiling.
  • Currently enrolled in a recognised post-matric course — class 11 onward, including ITI, polytechnic, diploma, graduation, post-graduation, engineering, medical, nursing, law and management programmes.
  • Not availing another scholarship scheme for the same course in the same academic session.
On the income limit — read this carefully. Current guidance for 2026-27 puts the ceiling at ₹3,00,000 per year across SC, ST, BC and EBC. You will also find ₹2,50,000 and ₹1,50,000 quoted on many websites — those figures come from earlier years and from the separate centrally-sponsored scheme, and they get copied forward without being updated. Confirm the current ceiling on the official portal notification before you get an income certificate made, because a certificate showing a figure above the ceiling cannot be undone later.

How much money do you actually get?

The scholarship has two components, and most students only think about the first:

  1. Tuition and compulsory fees — reimbursed up to a cap that depends on your course and on whether your institution is government or private-recognised.
  2. Maintenance or academic allowance — a separate amount toward living costs, books and materials. Hostellers receive more than day scholars.

These are reported figures for orientation only, not a guarantee. Course-wise caps are set by government notification and revised periodically. Your sanctioned amount depends on your course, your institution's status, your category, and whether you are a hosteller or day scholar. Treat the official notification as the authority.

Documents to arrange before you start

Do not open the form until you have all of these scanned and ready. Half-finished applications that never get submitted are a large share of the failures every year.

  • Bihar residence certificate — issued by the CO, SDO or DM.
  • Income certificate — issued for financial year 2026-27. An old certificate from a previous year is one of the most common rejection reasons.
  • Caste certificate — matching the category you are applying under.
  • College bonafide certificate with fee breakdown — it must itemise the fees, not merely state that you are enrolled.
  • Previous year marksheet.
  • Aadhaar card.
  • Bank passbook — an account in your own name, Aadhaar-seeded for DBT.
Kosi division tip: in Saharsa, Madhepura and Supaul, income and residence certificates are usually obtained through the RTPS counter at the block office or via the Circle Officer. Allow a genuine week or two — in August and September these counters are at their busiest, precisely because every student in the division needs the same certificate at the same time. Colleges affiliated to BN Mandal University sometimes want the bonafide certificate in a particular format, so ask your institution's scholarship nodal officer what they issue before you queue for it.

The application process, stage by stage

Your application moves through a fixed sequence. Knowing the sequence tells you who to chase when it stalls:

  1. Registration — create an account on the portal matching your category.
  2. Fill application — personal, academic and bank details.
  3. Upload documents — the list above.
  4. Aadhaar seeding status — the portal checks that your bank account is Aadhaar-linked for DBT.
  5. Finalise — until you finalise, nothing moves forward.
  6. Institute verification — your college or school verifies enrolment and fees.
  7. District verification — the district welfare office reviews it.
  8. State approval — final sanction.
  9. Payment — released through DBT to your bank account.

Stage 5 is where most self-inflicted failures happen. Students fill the form, upload documents, assume they are done, and never press finalise. An unfinalised application is treated as never submitted.

Checking your status — and when the money actually arrives

Log in to the same portal you applied on and open the application status page. What you see there tells you where your file is sitting. Two things are worth understanding:

  • Approval and payment are different stages. "State approved" does not mean the money has been sent. The transfer moves through DBT afterwards and can be tracked separately on PFMS.
  • If you are stuck at "Institution Verification Pending" for more than about 30 days, the file is sitting with your college, not with the government. Go to the college office in person and ask the scholarship nodal officer to forward it. This is the single most effective thing you can do, and no amount of refreshing the portal substitutes for it.

Why applications get rejected

Almost every rejection comes from a short list:

  • Name mismatch across Aadhaar, marksheet and bank account — even a spelling variation or a missing surname.
  • Income certificate from an earlier financial year rather than 2026-27.
  • Bank account not Aadhaar-seeded for DBT, so the transfer fails even after approval.
  • Applying on the wrong category portal.
  • Availing another scholarship for the same course in the same session.
  • Not correcting a flagged defect in time. If a defect is found you are given a short window — reported as 15 days — to correct and re-finalise. Miss it and the application is rejected outright.

That last one matters more than students realise. Once you have submitted, check the portal every week. A defect notice you never saw is, in the end, indistinguishable from never having applied.

District offices in the Kosi division

If your application is stuck at district verification, or you need help with certificates, the district administration websites carry current contact details for the welfare section:

  • Saharsa — saharsa.nic.in/welfare
  • Madhepura — madhepura.nic.in
  • Supaul — supaul.nic.in/contact-details

We link the official district pages rather than reproducing phone numbers here, because officer postings and numbers change through the year — and an out-of-date number wastes a trip you may have travelled a long way to make.

Frequently asked questions

What is the last date for Bihar Post Matric Scholarship 2026-27?

15 September 2026. Deadlines in this scheme have been extended in past years, but never plan around an extension — apply early and confirm the current date on the portal.

Is there any application fee?

No. There is no government fee. If a cyber café or agent tells you a fee must be paid to the portal, that is incorrect. They may charge their own service charge for filling the form, which is a separate matter between you and them.

Can I apply if I am studying outside Bihar?

Domicile is the governing condition rather than the location of your institution, but the rules for out-of-state institutions differ by category and course. Confirm on the portal for your specific case before applying.

My money has not arrived even though the status says approved. What now?

Check your DBT and Aadhaar seeding status at your bank first — that is the most common cause. If seeding is correct, track the payment on PFMS, then approach the district welfare office.

Can I take another scholarship at the same time?

No. Availing another scholarship for the same course in the same academic session makes your PMS application liable to rejection.

The bottom line

Apply early in the window rather than close to the deadline. Portal load rises sharply in the final days, and more importantly your application has to pass through your college and your district office before the state approves it — both of those take time you do not control. An application finalised in August has room to absorb a defect notice and a correction. One finalised on 14 September does not.

Get the three certificates first, check that your bank account is Aadhaar-seeded, confirm your category portal, then sit down and fill the form once, properly.
