Bihar PMS Status Check 2026: Track Your Scholarship and Find Out When the Money Arrives

Updated 30 August 2026 · Scholarships

Compiled 30 August 2026. Portal behaviour, status labels and payment cycles change between years. Confirm anything decision-critical on the official portal or with your district welfare office.

Two questions account for almost every message students in Saharsa, Madhepura and Supaul send about scholarships: where is my application, and paisa kab aayega.

They have different answers, and confusing them is why so many students wait months without acting. This guide covers both.

Step 1: check on the right portal

Your status lives on whichever portal you applied through:

  • BC and EBC students — pmsonline.bihar.gov.in
  • SC and ST students — scstpmsonline.bihar.gov.in
Official portals: pmsonline.bihar.gov.in and scstpmsonline.bihar.gov.in. Log in with your registration ID. No third-party site can show you your real status.

Keep your registration ID somewhere you will not lose it. Recovering it later, when a deadline is close, is far more painful than saving it now.

Step 2: understand what your status actually means

The stage you are stuck at tells you exactly who is holding your file — and therefore who you need to go and speak to.

StageWho holds itWhat you should do
Application not finalisedYouNothing has been submitted. Log in and finalise it — this is the most common silent failure.
Defective / objection raisedYouCorrect and re-finalise within the stated window, reported as 15 days. Miss it and the application is rejected.
Institute verification pendingYour college or schoolGo to the college office in person and ask the scholarship nodal officer to forward it.
District verification pendingDistrict welfare officeVisit or contact the district welfare section with your registration ID.
State approval pendingState departmentWait. This stage is processed in batches.
Approved / sanctionedPayment systemApproval is not payment. Move to the PFMS check below.
The single most useful thing on this page: if your status says "institution verification pending" and has said so for more than about 30 days, your file is sitting with your own college — not with the government. Refreshing the portal will never change it. One visit to the college office, asking the nodal officer to forward your application, usually will.

Step 3: if a defect is flagged

A defective status is not a rejection. It is a short, time-limited chance to fix something — and it is the stage where the most scholarships are quietly lost.

  1. Log in and read the exact objection. It will usually name the field or document.
  2. Correct it. Common defects are a name that does not match across Aadhaar, marksheet and bank account; an income certificate from the wrong financial year; and incorrect bank details.
  3. Re-finalise. Correcting without re-finalising does nothing at all.
  4. Confirm the status has moved on.

This is why you should check the portal weekly after submitting rather than waiting to be told. There is no guarantee you will be contacted, and a defect notice you never saw ends the same way as never having applied.

Step 4: approved, but no money — check PFMS

Once the state approves your scholarship, payment is made by Direct Benefit Transfer. That transfer is handled through the Public Financial Management System (PFMS), and it is tracked separately from the scholarship portal.

Payment tracking: pfms.nic.in — track by your bank account number or application reference.

If PFMS shows nothing, or shows a failed transfer, the cause is nearly always at the bank end rather than the scholarship end:

  • The account is not Aadhaar-seeded for DBT. This is by far the most common cause. Having an Aadhaar number linked to your account is not the same as the account being DBT-seeded. Ask your bank branch to confirm DBT seeding specifically.
  • Name mismatch between your Aadhaar and your bank record.
  • Account dormant or closed — common with accounts opened years earlier for a school scheme and never used since.
  • Wrong account number or IFSC entered in the application.

What to carry when you go in person

Whether you are visiting your college office or the district welfare office, take:

  • Your registration or application ID, written down.
  • A printout of the current status screen.
  • Photocopies of your Aadhaar, caste certificate, income certificate and bank passbook.
  • Originals, to show but not to hand over.

Go in the morning. In August and September these offices are dealing with the whole division at once.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check my Bihar Post Matric Scholarship status?

Log in with your registration ID on the portal you applied through — pmsonline.bihar.gov.in for BC and EBC students, or scstpmsonline.bihar.gov.in for SC and ST students — and open the application status page.

My status says approved but I have not received the money. Why?

Approval and payment are separate stages. Track the transfer on PFMS at pfms.nic.in. The most common cause of a failed transfer is a bank account that is not Aadhaar-seeded for DBT, which is different from simply having Aadhaar linked to the account.

What does defective status mean in PMS?

It means an objection has been raised and you have a short window, reported as 15 days, to correct the issue and re-finalise the application. If you correct it but do not re-finalise, nothing moves, and the application is rejected when the window closes.

My application has been stuck at institution verification for weeks. What do I do?

The file is with your college, not the government. Visit the college office in person and ask the scholarship nodal officer to forward it. This is the fastest route and there is no online substitute for it.

How long does the scholarship take to reach the bank account?

There is no fixed period. Payments are released in batches after state approval and move through DBT, so timing varies between cycles and categories. Track PFMS rather than relying on dates quoted on unofficial websites.

The bottom line

Check weekly, not once. Learn which stage you are at, because that tells you whether the problem is yours, your college's, or the district's. And if you have been approved but not paid, take your passbook to the bank and ask specifically whether the account is DBT-seeded — that one question resolves most cases.
